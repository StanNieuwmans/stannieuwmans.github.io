Each Assistant is a lantern with a Mood, set by what is really happening: never a guess, never decoration.

It answers, remembers and acts.

Everything happens in one Chat. Click the Assistant's face to see what it remembers, what it is working on, and what it does on a schedule.

One continuous Chat

No new threads to start, no history to lose. Each Assistant has exactly one Chat, and Search finds anything said in it.

Memory you can see

It records what it learns about you automatically, and every line is always visible, editable and deletable in the Library.

Goals with a budget

It pursues an objective over days, inside a spend cap per Goal and per day, with regular Check-ins. One Stop command halts them all.

Routines

Things it does on a schedule, like the Morning brief. Switch each one on or off, and set Quiet hours when it starts nothing.

Connect apps

Mail, calendar, files and more connect as Integrations over MCP. It takes a first look at each one and suggests what to do.

A Workspace of its own

It runs code and browses the web in a separate space that holds no credentials and cannot reach your Memory.

More than one Assistant

Give each Assistant its own name, colour, Memory and Goals. Put two to four of them in a Group chat; only the ones you @-mention answer.

You decide how much it may do alone.

Every action of every Integration has an Action tier. An Ask-first action waits for your Approval request; it expires after three days and is never executed late.

  • Free Just does it. Reading your calendar, searching your mail.
  • Notify Does it, then tells you in the Chat.
  • Ask first Asks before each one. Approve, deny, or say "tell me after" next time.
  • Never Off limits. Never cannot be configured away.

Bring your own model.

Baken needs a language model for two Model roles: the Chat model and the Goal model. Each gets its own provider and your own API key, so you pay the provider directly and choose where your Chat is processed.

You say where

Any other

Any server that speaks the same format as OpenAI. You tell Baken whether it is under EU jurisdiction.

Choose a provider outside the EU and Baken shows that the Instance is no longer EU-compliant, and why. You can change providers at any time.

Your data path, under EU jurisdiction.

An Instance is EU-compliant when its whole Controlled stack is under EU jurisdiction: compute, storage, model providers, Clients and Baken's own code. Choose one component that is not, and the status is lost, visibly.

Integrations are outside the Controlled stack

Connecting an app, such as your mail or calendar, does not change your Instance's status. But what the Assistant sends to that app goes to its service, under that service's jurisdiction. Every action still has an Action tier, so you decide what it may do there.

What you need

Baken itself costs nothing. You pay for your server, and for your model provider unless you run a model on your own server.

  • A server A Hetzner server takes about 10 minutes to set up. Any Ubuntu or Debian server works too.
  • A domain name Passkeys need one. No domain yet? The installer offers a free name that points to your server.
  • A model provider's API key Mistral by default. Or point Baken at a model on your own server and skip the key.

Use it from the web app on your phone, or the desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux.
